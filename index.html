The problem

Consent must satisfy regulators and people — today it satisfies neither.

Context

D4L Collect is moving from observational digital studies into clinical-research rigor — eCRF, ePRO, patient registry. eConsent is the participant's first interaction and the first trust-and-compliance priority named for this role.

Problem

Consent must be complete enough for regulators (ICH-GCP, EU CTR, GDPR Art. 9) yet understandable for real people. Today the long document wins and comprehension loses.

Early withdrawal & re-consent Ethics / audit risk Hidden coordinator burden from verbal re-explanation

How might we raise genuine comprehension across diverse participants without sacrificing completeness or adding site workload?

Discovery → Definition

Research approach, expected findings, and defining principles.

How I'd research it

  • Participants (primary), coordinators, PIs / regulatory & QA
  • Regulatory + health-literacy desk scan
  • Baseline comprehension measure — adapted QuIC on current flow
  • 8–12 discovery interviews across age, literacy & language incl. AT users
  • Moderated observation of live consent sessions

What I'd expect to learn

  • Consent reads as "terms & conditions" — skim and accept
  • "Data-fate" questions dominate trust: what data, who sees it, how long, can I withdraw
  • Comprehension is unequal across age, literacy & language
  • Coordinators are the hidden comprehension patch
  • Dense legal blocks aren't assistive-tech navigable

How that defines the problem

POV

People are asked to agree before they're equipped to understand.

Principles

  • Comprehension over completeness-as-default
  • Progressive disclosure
  • No dark patterns
  • Accessibility-native
  • Audit by design
↑ QuIC ↓ Drop-off ↓ Coordinator-assist WCAG 2.1 AA

Solution

Layered Living Consent — plain-language understanding on top of preserved regulatory text.

Step 0 · Entry Calm onboarding sets the study context before any legal text appears.
Step 1 · Understand Plain-language study overview with layered detail on demand.
Step 2 · Read & understand "Who can see your data" + a teach-back checkpoint. Calm amber clarification, never a red error.

4-step flow  —  Understand → Read & understand → Optional choices → Confirm & sign  +  living "My agreements" record

Trust by design

Two decisions that make GDPR-valid consent visible and withdrawal a standing right.

Step 3 · Optional choices All toggles default OFF with equal visual weight. Participation never depends on opting in — GDPR-valid, no nudging.
Step 4 · Confirm & sign Plain-language recap of exactly what's agreed, timestamped and versioned, exports to audit trail with zero extra coordinator effort. Withdrawal stays visible as an ongoing right.

Comprehension uplift ↓ Drop-off ↓ Coordinator-assist AA conformance

Why this role, why now

eConsent comprehension sits at the intersection of regulatory integrity and participant experience — exactly the space this role names. The design challenge isn't choosing between compliance and clarity; it's proving they're the same goal.

Regulation-as-design and WCAG 2.1 AA aren't constraints to route around here — they're the success criteria.
